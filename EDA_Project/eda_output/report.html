  5. Do categories differ in price, and are enough books per category to compare them?
  6. Is stock status related to price or rating?
  7. Do books with longer descriptions cost more or rate higher?

Q6-Q7. Relationships between variables

Hypothesis tests

Each test states a null hypothesis H0. p (Holm) adjusts for running several tests at once. A tiny p-value alone is not enough - check the effect size, because with many rows even trivial differences become 'significant'.

name H0 test n statistic p_value p_holm effect effect_size verdict note
Price differs by rating (Kruskal-Wallis) Price distribution is the same for every rating Kruskal-Wallis 200 0.5078 0.9727 1 0 negligible Fail to reject H0 (no evidence of an effect)
Price vs rating association (Spearman) No monotonic association between price and rating (rho = 0) Spearman rho 200 0.0151 0.832 1 0.0151 negligible Fail to reject H0 (no evidence of an effect)
Price differs by category (Kruskal-Wallis) Price distribution is the same in every category Kruskal-Wallis (15 categories, n>=5) 162 10.81 0.7006 1 0 negligible Fail to reject H0 (no evidence of an effect)
Price differs by stock status (Mann-Whitney U) Price distribution is the same for in-stock and out-of-stock books - 0 - - - - n/a SKIPPED: smaller group has only 0 rows
Rating vs stock status (chi-square) Rating and stock status are independent - 0 - - - - n/a SKIPPED: contingency table smaller than 2x2
Description length vs price (Spearman) No monotonic association between description length and price Spearman rho 199 0.07778 0.2749 1 0.07778 negligible Fail to reject H0 (no evidence of an effect)
Ratings are balanced (chi-square goodness-of-fit) All five ratings are equally common Chi-square GOF vs uniform 200 3 0.5578 1 0.1225 small Fail to reject H0 (no evidence of an effect)

0 of 5 tests rejected H0 after correction. No statistically supported relationships were found - consistent with these variables being unrelated in this sample.

Caveats: correlation is not causation; tests assume independent observations (books on one site are plausibly independent, but not guaranteed); assumptions of Kruskal-Wallis include similar distribution shapes across groups.
